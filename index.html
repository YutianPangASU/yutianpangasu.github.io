About

I am a postdoctoral research fellow in the Department of Aerospace Engineering & Engineering Mechanics at The University of Texas at Austin, working with Prof. John-Paul Clarke. My research connects artificial intelligence with air transportation: I develop decision-making methods for complex cyber-physical systems with humans in the loop, and I test them on the demanding problems of smart air traffic and logistics.

The work follows a recurring loop — build an understanding of a system from heterogeneous operational data; support decisions through optimization and simulation; then assess the risk and reliability that determine whether a method can be trusted in operation. The methods draw on operations research, statistical modeling, and reliability analysis, but they are built to be deployed rather than to stay on paper.

Before UT Austin, I earned my Ph.D. at Arizona State University working on a five-year NASA aviation data-science project, and spent a year at Thales building machine-learning systems for a certified air traffic management platform. I am also the co-founder and CTO of a healthcare agentic-AI startup in Austin.

Research

A recurring feature of my work is that it joins two traditions usually kept apart in transportation systems: high-fidelity modeling and simulation on one side, and data-driven learning and optimization on the other. Four threads run through the program:

Human–AI teaming in safety-critical operations

Language AI that interprets pilot–controller voice communication and links it to surface-movement collision risk; conformalized graph learning that returns calibrated, real-time estimates of controller cognitive workload; scheduling models that encode operator preferences as soft constraints.

System intelligence for networked operations

Spatio-temporal graph transformers for multi-aircraft trajectory prediction, physics-informed graph attention networks for airspace state forecasting, and data-driven recovery of governing equations for traffic-flow dynamics — with calibrated uncertainty through Bayesian treatment.

Scheduling & logistics under uncertainty

Machine-learning-enhanced aircraft landing scheduling, geometry-based trajectory optimization for terminal-area arrivals, dynamic airspace sectorization, and last-mile / same-day delivery routing that respects vehicle capacity and time guarantees.

Trustworthy methods across the model–data spectrum

Probabilistic deep learning, reinforcement learning, and NLP on the data-driven end; nonlinear and integer optimization, discrete-event simulation, and reliability modeling on the model-driven end — and a hybrid middle that uses simulation to train, surrogates to accelerate, and operational rules to keep outputs interpretable.

Current directions

  • Human-centered language and intent understanding for safety-critical operations: automated root-cause analysis of safety reports, compliance monitoring, and real-time voice-based support that operators can check.
  • Hybrid optimization for scheduling and logistics: uncertainty-aware re-optimization and resilience under disruption, coupling simulation with optimization for production and logistics networks.
  • Reliable end-to-end decision-making for highly automated systems: decentralized perception, prediction, and planning under sensing and communication uncertainty, alongside human-AI teaming.

Earlier work

My Ph.D. work at ASU built Bayesian deep-learning models for aircraft trajectory prediction under weather uncertainty — including the B-STAR spatio-temporal graph transformer and some of the first probabilistic deep models for terminal-area trajectory prediction — alongside graph learning for airspace state and controller workload. At Thales, I moved models of this kind from prototype into a platform that had to meet government certification, which meant confronting the computing, security, and reliability questions that surface once a learning system enters production.
