TRACK · WORLD MODELS

World model track: from LLM training to world models

Walks through the main 2026 routes for world models, then builds one: a context-conditioned, action-controllable multi-agent world model, post-trained with RL on verifiable rewards and tested in closed loop.

Prerequisites:

The aim is to understand the whole pipeline well enough to set direction, and to be hands-on with post-training and the infrastructure it depends on. At the frontier, the post-training bottleneck has moved from the algorithms themselves to environment and reward design, the stability of RL systems, and the rigor of evaluation. World models are no different: the hard parts are data, rewards, and closed-loop evaluation. Stages marked ★ are the core ones, and every stage states how deep to go.

STARTStarting point

  • Comfortable with deep learning, probabilistic modeling, and sequence models
  • Experience with time series or spatiotemporal modeling helps
  • Have not trained a large model from scratch or run a training cluster

To fill in: first-hand experience with pretraining and data; GPUs, distributed training, and inference systems; the engineering details of RL post-training; and the mainstream approaches to world models.

GOALWhere it ends

  • Explain the trade-offs at every step of the LLM training pipeline
  • Build your own training, rollout, and evaluation stack
  • Build an action-conditioned world model of a domain you know, on public data
  • Post-train it with RL on separate verifiable rewards and validate it in closed loop in a simulator

The first two come from the main tracks (M, I); the last two from the World model track (W).

RULESFiltering the noise

  1. Primary sources: tech report appendices; the source code of nanochat, open-instruct, verl, TRL, and torchtitan
  2. People who actually train models: Smol Training Playbook, Ultra-Scale Playbook, Interconnects, Thinking Machines, Karpathy, the Marin dev logs
  3. Courses: CS336, rlhfbook, How to Scale Your Model
  4. X, Medium, newsletters, surveys: leads only, not evidence

Treat with suspicion: RL results shown only on Qwen; gains from a single seed or a small benchmark; recipes without code or hyperparameters.
